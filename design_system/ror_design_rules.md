# ROR Design Rules for Content Packs

Source: Gorilla Studio identity direction and `colors_and_type.css`.

## Core Visual Language

Rock On Ruby should feel bold, giftable, cheeky and useful. The brand does not need soft lifestyle styling to be warm.

Default design traits:

- Beige or cream canvas
- Black ink
- Hard black outlines
- Flat colour blocks
- Offset block shadows
- Big type
- Strong product close-ups
- One or two loud accents, not a muddy rainbow
- Sharp, practical layouts that feel shoppable

Avoid:

- Generic pastel Instagram templates
- Overly soft script-heavy styling
- Corporate minimalism
- Blurry mood imagery
- Stock-photo energy
- Designs that make the product hard to inspect

## Type Direction

Use the design system as a voice selector:

- Druk Wide: big ROR statements, campaign headers, poster moments.
- Druk: tall condensed editorial display, bold hooks, attitude-led content.
- Sharp Grotesk: everyday headings and body copy.
- Cooper Black: retro, warm, funny, softer nostalgia.
- Prohibition or Futura Condensed: patch, badge, pub, dad, sport and poster energy.
- Old English: varsity, heritage, year tops and bold personalised moments.
- ITC Garamond: quieter story-led editorial moments.

## Palette Direction by Moment

### Father's Day

Use beige, black, navy, army green, orange and dark teal.

Feel:

- Practical
- Bold
- Giftable
- A bit dry and funny
- Less pink-led unless the product itself needs it

Good for:

- Caps
- Slogan sweatshirts
- BBQ or food angles
- Dad gift guides
- Last-minute gifting

### Mother's Day and Gifts for Mum

Use beige, cream, black, pink, bright red, baby pink and butter.

Feel:

- Warm
- Personal
- Thoughtful
- Not twee
- Still a bit funny

Good for:

- Personalised sweatshirts
- Tote bags
- Make-up bags
- Gift guides
- Story-led emails

### Valentine's and Galentine's

Use beige, black, bright red, pink, hot pink and baby pink.

Feel:

- Cheeky
- Warm
- More mate-to-mate than romantic cliche
- Anti-boring

Good for:

- Matching gifts
- Personalised accessories
- Friendship gifting
- Funny slogans

### Festival and Summer

Use beige, black, bright orange, bright blue, pink, bright yellow and teal.

Feel:

- Loud
- Fun
- Useful
- Slightly chaotic in a good way
- Product-in-action

Good for:

- Caps
- Totes
- Travel pouches
- Festival group gifting
- Holiday packing content

### Birthday and Milestone Gifting

Use beige, black, bright red, pink, navy, burgundy, gold and Old English or varsity type where useful.

Feel:

- Personal
- Celebratory
- Not childish
- Clear gift intent

Good for:

- Year sweatshirts
- Birthday hoodies
- Birth-year products
- Gift bundles

### Christmas

Use cream, black, bright red, dark red, forest green, army green and gold.

Feel:

- Gift-guide friendly
- Clear
- Warm
- Shoppable
- Slightly chaotic but controlled

Good for:

- Gift guides
- Christmas jumpers
- Biscuit range
- Stocking filler content
- Last-order-date content

### Back To School and Autumn

Use beige, black, burgundy, navy, forest green, butter and orange.

Feel:

- Reset
- Useful
- Organised but still playful
- Practical gifting

Good for:

- Totes
- Personalised accessories
- Teacher gifts
- Autumn sweatshirts

## Content Pack Design Output

Every content pack should include:

- Palette: 4 to 6 named colours from the design system.
- Type mood: one or two type directions.
- Layout notes: blocks, posters, badges, editorial, product grid or story frames.
- Shot notes: what to film or photograph.
- Canva notes: practical guidance Bethan can follow.
- Avoid notes: what would make the piece feel wrong.

## Example

Father's Day cap pack:

Palette: beige, black, navy, army green and orange.

Type mood: Prohibition or Futura Condensed for badge energy, Sharp Grotesk for supporting copy.

Layout notes: Use hard black outlines, one orange CTA strip and close product crops. Keep the product large enough to read the embroidery.

Shot notes: Film packing, cap close-ups, embroidery detail and a quick gift-box finish.

Avoid notes: Avoid pink-heavy styling, soft florals, sentimental stock-photo cues or tiny unreadable product shots.
